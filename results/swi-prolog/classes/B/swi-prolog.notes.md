## Difficult token decisions

**Arithmetic evaluators and expressibility.** I classed the arithmetic computations in `sum`, `sum_known`, `product`, `product_known`, and `factor` as P. Computing any known-place direction of the primitive arithmetic relation is still that relation, including the zero-factor case and exact integer divisibility check. This follows the mode-faithful reading of rules 3 and 8 and the evaluator principle made explicit in rule 19. There is no accumulator, problem-specific held-relation data structure, extra state argument, separate splitting/reversing algorithm, or moved conjunct here. Thus there are no R tokens in these texts.

The `nonvar` calls and cuts are C under rules 1 and 2: they dispatch on instantiation and commit to an operational mode, rather than testing a mathematical case's pattern. The zero-case cut in `factor(C,0,_)` is included; when its divisor is unknown the head can bind it to zero before the arithmetic test errors. This observable binding matters to outcomes. `when/2`'s wrapper, wake condition and closing delimiter are C, but its nested arithmetic evaluator is P (rule 10). Separating commas belong to the following construct (rule 20). SLG declarations and the occurs-check flag directive are C; the latter can change meaning as well as execution and therefore uses rule 2.

There is a genuine scope ambiguity here: rule 19 is introduced specifically for imperative holding code. I have not treated that as an instruction to class native Prolog instantiation tests as R; rules 1 and 2 specify C for operational mode selection. Nor have I treated every arithmetic helper name as a new mathematical definition requiring R. A stricter reading of “definitions, cases ... the problem does not give” could disagree with this application of rules 3 and 8. The token spans identify every affected construct so this disagreement can be localized.

I judge all these subproblems expressible, in the specification's sense of transcribable definitions. This is separate from the ability of the supplied transcription to execute a partially instantiated query or finish its search. Plain/tabling arithmetic errors and backwards reverse's nontermination/resource error therefore remain outcome failures without becoming different expressibility classes. If expressibility were intended to require an operationally successful solver for each presented partial query, the specification should say so explicitly; that stronger reading would change the plain/tabling P02 assessment.

**Harness and constant plumbing.** `unknowns` and fact-form `query` declarations, and the heads of `stage/3` through `:-`, are H under harness README sections 2.1 and 12. Stage bodies are classified independently. Constant facts and deterministic `memberchk/2` extraction of a delivered stage constant are P: they supply the problem's values and do not select among alternative solutions. The lookups are listed as external `library(lists)` operations, with rule 9's equivalent-built-in reading; they are not mem-relation problem operations. `use_module(library(clpfd))` is P as the declaration enabling the primitive integer constraint notation, with external-library status under rule 12. No program operation counters are present.

**S coverage.** Session loads the phase-4 wait-for-undefined-procedure code, but the supplied frozen token files and TEXTS manifest cover problem programs only. They contain no frozen token stream for `harnesses/swi-prolog/S/session-wait.pl`. I did not re-tokenize that file or fabricate indices for it. Its declared phase-4 size is 158 tokens, a declaration rather than this classifier's frozen-token count. It is an S mechanism of the configuration, unused by P01–P03, with an H wait call nested inside it. Zero S in the supplied-program totals must not be interpreted as absence of supporting code from the session configuration.

## Difficult outcome decisions

**Decoding open lists and free variables.** P01b's `T=[1,2|S]` is read as the solved form `T=<1,2>++S`, with S a finite sequence in the problem's value encoding. The relation has been decided by construction, so no conc goal remains suspended. An improper host term, for example `[1,2|an_atom]`, is not a value of the mathematical finite-sequence encoding. The source has no explicit proper-list-tail check; if host terms outside that encoding are intended to count as extra reported problem values, this would instead be an unsound stage and a missing domain guard. I record this interpretation explicitly rather than silently adding a guard to the program. For P02, the problem explicitly makes all unknowns integers; a free W is an arbitrary integer, not an imposed finite domain. None of these arithmetic programs supplies a finite interval. CLPFD's name does not establish such a restriction.

**Observed bindings are reports.** I applied SPECIFICATION Outcomes 1 literally: a binding readable by `observe()` counts as reported even if no answer was delivered through `answers()`.

* Primary P01c is cyclic and unsound under the finite reading, complete under the rational reading. This also applies to tabling: although its table reset raises an acyclic-term error before delivering an answer, the record's exception hook reads `X=[1|X]`. Its execution state is error, independently of the rationally correct observed binding. The occurs-check named variant exhausts with no answers: an explicit empty set, complete under the finite reading and unsound under the rational reading.
* In plain/tabling P02 orders beginning with R2, the record observes `U=0` at the error. With V and W free and no recorded arithmetic residual, that solved form admits invalid triples: `(0,1,0)` violates R2. In order R3R2R1 stage 2 the observed `U=2` likewise leaves a report admitting `(2,0,1)`, which violates R2. These are unsound/error, rather than none-reported/error. Where the error snapshot is merely `true` with no binding, no answer and no held residual, I use none reported; that text is not evidence the posed arithmetic relation was retained.
* In non-tabled P03f, the collected answer `A=[3,2,1]` is correct. At budget exhaustion the hook also reports a proper list skeleton of length 182, all elements free. Every completion has the wrong length. Counting this observed binding as a report makes the whole report unsound/budget. Representation is explicit on the more explicit valid collected answer; the invalid held solved form and its counterexample are recorded alongside it. Looking only at collected answers would instead give complete/budget, exactly as the P03 problem's example anticipates. The problem's example and the general observed-binding rule are therefore classification-sensitive in this actual record. I followed the general rule rather than adding an exception for speculative search bindings. A rule that distinguishes tentative search bindings from conclusions would resolve this tension.

**Not reached.** Eleven later stages in plain and eleven in tabling never execute because an earlier arithmetic stage errors during replay. Their axes are null, and their status is not reached (Amendments 3.2). Some `restart_cost_source` strings incorrectly say replay “stopped by budget”; the exception and execution-state evidence instead show an instantiation error. The stopping stage comes from that source's entry/replay information, and the stopping cause comes from the actual error. I did not class these later stages as independent errors or as their own unsound reports.

**Residual equivalence, identity and size.** The coroutine delays denote the arithmetic relation they will decide on ground integer places, rather than a successful assertion that the wake condition is already true. On ground integer substitutions, their arithmetic evaluator accepts exactly addition or multiplication; the control condition only decides when it executes. CLPFD's held relations have the same integer denotation. R1 alone and R2 alone are reduced residuals. R2 with R3 has U=2 and the single reduced residual `2*W=V`. Arithmetic expression terms are not among the specification's solved-form terms, so I did not call the last relation a solved form.

R1 together with R2 is complete but reducible: eliminating V gives `U*(W+1)=16`; the exact divisor argument gives the ten stated triples, all of which can be expressed with zero relations left to decide. Neither the coroutine nor CLPFD program reduces to those explicit alternatives. This accounts for nine reducible entries: P02a and two P02b orders, in three configurations. The other 21 residual entries are reduced. No residual is classified as inconsistency undetected.

Residual sizes count primitive relations left to decide and occurrences of unknowns, not output characters, solver-internal watcher nodes or `when` syntax. The input is all information through that stage before simplification. Thus R1+R2 has input/residual size (2,5); R2+R3 has input (2,4), one decided binding and residual (1,2). The identity flag uses the same decoded mathematical comparison: untouched R1/R2 relations are identical to posed information, whereas substitution of U=2 changes it. This is an explicit interpretation of “identical”; a byte-for-byte rendering comparison would classify the coroutine wrapper differently without showing a mathematical simplification.

**Suspension, alternatives and missing counts.** Attributed-variable counts and projected residual-goal counts are both retained, in their recorded units. They are not interchangeable: the initial coroutine product has two attributed variables but one projected residual goal, whereas CLPFD's corresponding relation has three attributed variables and one projected goal. Alternative frames are kept for backtracking. A null count is unavailable, not zero. A finished query can have reported earlier answers even when no bindings survive exhaustive backtracking at the boundary. Session's answers on intermediate stages come from separate prefix runs; its observation reads the incremental engine. The harness waiting for the next stage is not an extra unresolved problem relation. No manual actions or withdrawals of an earlier stage's conclusion are recorded. Refining a free variable or an alias is not withdrawal.

## Measurement-mode differences

All cost-mode classifications agree with behaviour. Four storage records differ: P03f in plain, coroutines, constraints and session. Each contains an unreadable/interleaved `at_exception` event and no valid computation-end event. The synthetic record state `[error]` is explicitly marked undetermined and is not a system error. I use null execution state for this mode and preserve the raw synthetic label and harness fault as evidence. Stage-end step totals and budget-exhausted status are unavailable; the valid first-answer latency of 19 inferences remains available. The record's reused last-answer count of 19 cannot establish the stage's total work.

The only readable answer in those storage records is the unique correct reverse; their available report is therefore complete/explicit. The invalid 182-element boundary binding visible in behaviour/cost is unavailable there. Each difference gives both classifications and the evidence limitation. No missing boundary value is treated as proof that the system actually avoided that binding, finished, or raised a model error.

The four behaviour/cost P03f budget records report 1,000,009 inferences against a 1,000,000 budget: a recorded overrun of nine. Amendment 2 describes slack of at most eight. I preserve the recorded figures and flag this numerical discrepancy without changing the independently recorded budget state. Tabling P03f instead raises `resource_error(private_table_space)` before an answer; its missing step and boundary-state measurements remain unavailable.
