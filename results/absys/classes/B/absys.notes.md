**Equality identification versus a residual.** P01a stages 1 and 2 are the hardest representation decision. The machine holds one and two suspended equations, respectively; it does not implement equality by replacing the names with a single shared machine unknown. Nevertheless the report explicitly identifies the unknowns, and identification is expressly admitted by the definition of *solved form*. I classify the displayed `x=y` and `x=y=z` as complete solved forms with suspended execution. The suspended process counts remain one and two. There is no arithmetic or user-defined relation left to solve in the report. A name not yet occurring, such as z at stage 1, is free. All three names subsequently become 2.

The same decision applies to P01b stage 1: `s=m₂`, `t=[1;2&m₂]` is a solved form consisting of identification and an open sequence term. Eliminating the local m₂ yields exactly `t=⟨1,2⟩++s`. The report still holds one suspended equality process, and stage 2 propagates `[3]` into the tail. I have not silently changed that operational observation into native variable aliasing. A stricter reading of “no relation remains to be decided” that treats every stored equality process as a residual would instead label these nine rows residual/reducible; that is a real ambiguity between the solved-form and operational wording. My choice follows the explicit permission for identification, and keeps the execution axis separate.

**Sequence-domain interpretation.** I read the open-tail list notation as the specification's sequence construction: its tail is a sequence, and `++` is defined only on sequences. Under that reading the P01b solved form is equivalent for every finite sequence s. The base case uses `q=r`, with no separate `sequence(q)` predicate. The permitted evidence does not specify or test what happens if an open tail is later bound to an atom or integer. I therefore make no claim that such an unrun case would be rejected by the implementation. If the report were instead interpreted as unrestricted cons terms admitting improper tails, P01b stage 1 would over-admit nonsequences. That interpretation would require revisiting both equivalence and the omission rule for the base case. This domain issue deserves explicit clarification in the notation/representation contract; it was not resolved by running a probe or reading system source outside the permitted list.
