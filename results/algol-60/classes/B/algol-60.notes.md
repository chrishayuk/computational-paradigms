## Token decisions requiring judgment

- **Held equations and arithmetic relations:** rule 19 decides. Knownness tests, retained-relation flags, changed/pass state, the problem-specific scan and its calls are R. The interfaces of the knownness-aware evaluators are R because they belong to this holding mechanism. Their computations of a value, updates marking that value known, and tests of fully known relations are P, including multiplication's zero and divisibility cases. These branches are classed regardless of whether this batch reaches them. Relation value slots and the inconsistency verdict are P declarations; knownness/holding declarations are R. A chained initialization of the holding flags, including its initial inconsistency flag, is treated as a single R initialization construct.
- **R rather than S or C:** these scans handle only the problem's own equalities, conc, or R1/R2/R3. Rule 19 expressly makes such holding R. No reusable relation interpreter, general unifier, search engine or scheduler occurs, and phase 4 lists no S mechanism. The propagation loop is part of the R holding formulation. There is no additional purely operational control construct to count as C. This explains the zero C and S totals; it does not attribute native suspension to ALGOL.
- **Sequence encoding:** `cons`, input-list construction, head/tail arrays, allocation and the required equality operation are P under the systems file and rule 15. Their loops traverse supplied data rather than generate unstated candidate values. The first initialization of `top` is required encoding initialization, not a later store-reuse reset under rule 16. Where a chain initializes `top` and observation counters together, the allocator target is P and the nested counter initialization is H under rules 10 and 13.
- **Separate directions:** `split`, `strip`, `prefix`, `lastsplit` and `unrev` have R computational constructs, including case guards, by rules 3 and 14. Path/depth state is R. `same` in P03d is used only for that separate direction and is R by rule 15; `same` in P01 is a problem encoding operation and P. The shared `cons`/`readseq` definitions remain P because they also construct the input encoding; their uses inside a separate direction are R with that computation. P03f moves the conc-derived `lastsplit` before recursive reversal: one conjunct move under rules 4 and 8, with no added move token.
- **Nested H:** printing procedures and conditional printing of already computed state are H under rule 13, including sequence/path printers. Observation counters, including counts used only to print whether any answer occurred, are H. Inside a direction-specific R procedure I retain H for these nested print/counter constructs and R for the surrounding case and recursion, applying rule 10. No output adapter here chooses a different algorithm to derive an answer.
- **Declarations and punctuation:** individual declarators are separate role-bearing constructs. A shared type introducer belongs to the first declarator; the common suffix of a combined array declaration is retained with its final declarator. Thus P03b's `hd, tl, path[... ]` has P head/tail declarators and an R path declarator including that suffix. This mixed declaration has no unique lexical allocation in the rules; the chosen convention is explicit and the same in both spellings. Semicolons and commas between constructs follow rule 20. Block/conditional/loop delimiters follow their introducing construct by rule 10. Procedure end identifiers present in the frozen tables are covered with their closing procedure; I did not remove them or otherwise correct the tokenizer.
- **Shared texts:** all aliases in `TEXTS.tsv` were checked against the token stream of their first path. The six P02b orders share text within each spelling, and x1/x1sim share text. No S-mechanism or core difference changes a token class between x1 and x1sim. Only the 24 first paths are therefore present in `tokens`; their classes apply to every listed alias.

The most significant potential rule ambiguity is whether rule 14's “R throughout” overrides the explicitly nested H adapters/counters. I read it as overriding P case-head/encoding exemptions, while rules 10 and 13 still apply to independent observation constructs. The converse reading would change token totals. I identify no rule as demonstrably wrong.

## Outcome decisions requiring judgment

**An equation held is not an identification.** P01a stages 1 and 2 retain separate unknown flags and equality flags, and wait for values; they do not merge unknown cells. Their residuals are complete but reducible: identifying the unknowns gives a smaller solved form. At stage 3 all three reported values are 2. The unbound names in an equality residual have the problem's full value domain. An inactive integer value slot is not a reported assertion that its unknown ranges only over integers. The program's comment about the eventual integer value is not used to narrow the mathematical valid outcomes.

**P01b stage 1:** the held conc relation is equivalent to `t=<1,2>++s` over the full finite-sequence domain. It is reducible because the latter is a solved form. The program has not constructed this open result: it waits for s. At stage 2 it computes both required values, with one base and two recursive applications of conc. The fixed allocation of 1000 cells can limit executable inputs, but the reported residual imposes no length bound on s. I therefore do not invent a finite-domain restriction in its denotation. For P01a/b the cost records give whole-stage costs; latency to an individual binding is unavailable.

**P01c has two judgments for the same physical stage.** The retained equation `x=<1>++x` denotes no finite solution, by the length contradiction, so its finite judgment is complete/residual/inconsistency undetected. It is not an unsound explicit finite answer, and it does not report that there are no solutions. Over rational trees the same condition denotes exactly the infinite all-1 sequence, so it is complete/residual/reducible. It does not construct or present a cyclic value. Neither configuration supplies a unifier or an occurs check.

The single-stage P01c is a boundary case for the specification's requirement that a residual be held and acted on later: there is no later stage in this instance. I accept it as a held residual because `a1`, `r1` and the unknown flag persist in actual program state and `examine1` is a real evaluator of that state, rather than an unconditional echo of input. Its lack of reduction and absence of a later event are recorded. A literal requirement that a later stage actually occur would prevent residual reporting for single-stage problems as well; I regard that wording as ambiguous.

**P02's finite intermediate set:** R1 and R2 are retained, rather than solved jointly. Eliminating v gives `u*(w+1)=16`; u must be a nonzero divisor of 16. `classify.py` derives exactly the ten triples listed in the problem. Its finite divisor calculation is an equivalence proof with a mathematically justified bound, not a restriction of the program's integer domain. The residual is complete but reducible to those explicit triples. Neither ten live alternatives nor ten enumerated answers were held by the program.

With only R3, `u=2` and free v/w is a solved form. With R1 and R3, `u=2,v=14` and free w is a solved form. With R2 and R3, `u=2` and `w*2=v` is a complete reduced residual: arithmetic multiplication is not a term constructor in the specification's solved-form language. R1 alone and R2 alone are reduced arithmetic residuals. All six P02b orders reach the same final triple, and P02c also reports that triple. The JSON preserves cumulative examination counts and records the final stage's source-inferred examination order, distinguishing it from a recorded native trace.

**Residual identity and size:** `identical_to_posed` compares the accumulated mathematical information before evaluation, ignoring whitespace, numeric padding, printed unknown labels and the display order of conjunctions. Thus the unchanged R1/R2 pair is identical even when it prints R1 before an earlier-arriving R2. The substituted R2/R3 residual is not identical. If “identical” means byte identity or presentation-order identity instead, that flag is ambiguous for R2-before-R1. Sizes give undecided primitive relations and their unknown occurrences, with bound unknowns separately. Literal sequence structure is a term, not an additional relation; named defined conc is counted as a relation rather than expanding its recursive definition. P01c is one recursive equality with two occurrences of x. No compound arithmetic here needs fresh intermediate unknowns. Solved bindings are not relations left to decide. Sizes are `null` when the chosen representation is not residual.

Every P03 direction finishes after its last answer, including P03f. There is no budget, timeout, error or retained-alternative outcome in these records. P03a/e are expressible transcriptions; the other directions require the named R computations.
